## From Learning Phase to Profit

New ad sets start in Meta's learning phase, where cost per result runs higher while the algorithm gathers data. We tracked this phase separately, so spend was judged on the right timeline instead of being cut too early.

Even during learning, several ad sets hit strong returns (Retargeting B at 9.99x, Summer Derma Roller at 6.50x), a sign that the creative and audiences matched from the start.

## What Drove the Results

### Test first, then scale.

We tested each core product across interest-based, city-targeted, lookalike and open audiences before raising spend. Lookalike and city-targeted audiences won consistently, and most products had a clear winning audience within the first month.

### Give the learning phase time.

We didn't judge new campaigns before Meta had enough data. Several performed strongly even during that window.

### Scale what works.

Once the winners were clear, we ran a seasonal push across nearly every product line, mixing new and retargeting audiences. We then rebuilt the account around the strongest campaigns while keeping budget aside for testing.

### Prove demand with a flash sale.

A one-day promotion drove sales, orders and traffic well above the brand's daily average, at a higher conversion rate: evidence that the audience we had built was warm and ready to buy.

### Let Instagram lead.

Instagram consistently outperformed Facebook, and social was the top source of both traffic and sales, ahead of direct and search. That fits a visual, texture-driven beauty brand.

## Summary

Over four months, Amira Essence held a 5–10x ROAS while spend increased, across ice rollers, colour cosmetics and derma devices, and across open, interest, lookalike and retargeting audiences. The structured testing in the first months decided what we scaled in September.

Results reflect this client's account during the period shown. Outcomes depend on product, market and budget; past results don't guarantee future performance.
